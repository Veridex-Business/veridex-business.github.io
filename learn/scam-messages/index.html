Lesson 2: Spotting scam messages

What you'll learn

How to spot a message that might be a scam, and the calm, safe thing to do.

Steps

1. Most messages are fine

You don't need to worry about every message. You only need to slow down when something feels odd.

2. Know the signs

Take care if a message:

  • rushes you ("Act now!")
  • says you've won a prize you never entered
  • asks for money, a password or a PIN
  • has a link (words you tap to open a web page)
  • comes from someone you don't know

3. Look at a made-up example

Made-up example of a fake text (written for this page, not real)

"Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay €1.99 now or it will be returned: parcel-help.example.com"

It rushes you. It asks for money. It has a link. That's three signs.

4. Don't tap the link

You don't need to open it to check it. Just leave it.

5. Check another way

If a message says it's from your bank or a company, contact them using a number you already trust. For your bank, use the phone number on the back of your bank card. Then delete the message.

Try it

Read these three made-up messages. Which ones need Stop, Check, Ask?

  • A: "Hi Mam, see you Sunday for dinner. Love, your son."
  • B: "You've won €500! Claim today only: prize.example.com"
  • C: "Your account is locked. Reply with your PIN to unlock it."

Answer: B and C. B rushes you and has a link. C asks for your PIN. Nobody genuine will ask for your PIN in a message.

Stop, Check, Ask

If a message feels odd:

  1. Stop. Don't tap the link. Don't reply.
  2. Check another way. Use a number you already trust, like the one on the back of your bank card.
  3. Ask someone you trust before you do anything.

If you think you may have shared details, ring your bank on the number on the back of your card, or contact your local Garda station. It's not your fault, and it's always fine to ask for help.

  • Most messages are fine.
