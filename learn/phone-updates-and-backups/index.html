Lesson 6: Keeping your phone safe and your photos backed up

What you'll learn

How to keep your phone up to date, and how to keep a spare copy of your photos.

Steps

1. Say yes to updates

An update (a free improvement from the company that made your phone or computer) fixes problems and helps keep you safe. When your phone offers one, it's a good idea to say yes.

2. Get updates only from your phone's own Settings

Real updates are found in Settings (the gear or cog picture). Never update from a pop-up or a link in a text.

A real warning on your phone or computer never asks you to ring a phone number.

Made-up example of a fake update pop-up (written for this page, not real)

"WARNING! Your phone is out of date. Tap here to update now: phone-fix.example.com"

It rushes you, and it isn't in your Settings. Close it, or go back.

3. Update your apps from the official app store

Apps (programs on your phone) get updates too. Get them from the official app store: the App Store on an iPhone, or Google Play on most other phones. Apps there are checked, but a fake can still slip in, so look at the app's name and who made it before you install.

4. Let updates run overnight

Updates can take a while. Plug your phone in to charge and connect to your home Wi-Fi (your home internet). Then let it update while you sleep.

5. Keep a backup of your photos

A backup is a spare copy of your photos, kept somewhere other than your phone. If your phone is lost or broken, your photos are still safe.

6. Turn on photo backup

On an iPhone: open Settings, tap your name, tap iCloud, then Photos, and turn it on. On most other phones: open the Google Photos app, tap your picture or initial at the top, then Photos settings, then Backup, and turn it on. Or ask a family member to help copy your photos to a computer or a memory stick (a small storage stick that plugs into a computer). Check now and then that it's still working.

Careful: with some photo backups, deleting a photo on your phone deletes the backup copy too. If you delete one by mistake, look in Recently Deleted straight away. For photos you treasure, also keep a copy on a computer or memory stick.

The free space is limited. When it's full, new photos stop backing up, and you'll be asked to clear some space or pay for more.

Try it

Open Settings on your phone. Look for "Software update" or "Update". On some phones it's inside "System" or "General". See if your phone says it's up to date. If an update is waiting, you can do it tonight while your phone charges.

Stop, Check, Ask

If a pop-up, text or call says your phone needs fixing or updating:

  1. Stop. Don't tap the pop-up or the link. Close it, or go back.
  2. Check in your phone's own Settings. Real updates are found there.
  3. Ask someone you trust.

If someone calls, texts or pops up out of the blue offering to fix your phone or computer, hang up or close it. Never let someone who contacts you out of the blue take control of your phone or computer. Only allow remote help if you contacted a support service you trust yourself, using a number you found yourself, not one from a pop-up, a text or a search ad. Your bank will never ask to control your phone or computer.

If you've already let someone in, turn off the Wi-Fi or switch the device off. Then ring your bank on the number on the back of your card.
