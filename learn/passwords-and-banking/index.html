Lesson 3: Passwords and safe banking

What you'll learn

How to keep your accounts locked with good passwords, and a few safe habits for online banking.

Steps

1. Long passwords are strong

A good password is long. Three random words joined together are easy to remember and hard to guess.

Made-up example password (for this page only, never use it)

teapot-river-violin

Think of your own words quietly, in private. Never say a password out loud.

2. Use different passwords for important things

Your email and your bank should each have their own password. That way, one lost "key" doesn't open every door.

3. A password manager can remember them for you

A password manager (a locked notebook on your phone) stores your passwords safely. You only need to remember one main password. You can decide later if you want one.

4. Turn on a 2-step check

A 2-step check (sometimes called "2FA") is a second lock. After your password, the account sends a code to your phone. Never tell anyone that code.

5. Bank safely

Use your bank's own app from the official app store, or type your bank's web address yourself. Never log in from a link in a message. Log out when you're finished.

6. Your PIN and passwords are yours alone

Only you type your PIN and passwords. Never tell anyone, even someone who says they're from your bank.

Try it

Open Settings on your phone. Find "Security", "Password" or "Screen lock". Just look at what's there. Don't change anything unless you want to. If you do, type it yourself, in private.

Stop, Check, Ask

If anyone asks for your PIN, password or a code:

  1. Stop. Don't type it, say it or send it.
  2. Check. Hang up. Then ring your bank on the number on the back of your bank card. On a landline, wait until you hear the dial tone first, or use a different phone.
  3. Ask someone you trust.
